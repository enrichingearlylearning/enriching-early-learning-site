Welcome back to [Tanya and Tanmay's Corner](https://www.enrichingearlylearning.com/tanya-and-tanmays-corner-a-fun-filled-exciting-parenting-journey/)!

In our last post, we shared how Tanya and Tanmay kept [Sophie and Ryan's routines steady during the holidays](https://www.enrichingearlylearning.com/toddler-preschooler-routines-during-holidays/). This time the story is a little different. Instead of going to someone else's home, the whole family was coming to theirs.

That year, Tanya and Tanmay offered to host the big holiday dinner. Twenty guests, seven children, lots of food and one small house. Ryan was 3 and Sophie was 7. Tanya was excited, but she was also a little worried. She knew her own home would not feel like home to the children that evening.

So she did one thing that made the whole night easier. She set up a **calm corner**.

## What is a calm corner?

A calm corner is a small, cozy space where a child can go when everything feels like too much. It is not a time-out spot and it is never used as a punishment. It is simply a safe place to slow down, take a break and feel better before joining back in.

Many early years classrooms already have one. Tanya had seen how well it worked in Ryan's daycare, so she decided to bring the idea home for the party.

## How did Tanya and Tanmay set up their calm corner?

They chose a corner in the children's bedroom, away from the kitchen and the living room where most of the noise would be. It did not cost much. Most of the things were already at home.

**Here is what went into it:**

- **Soft seating:** Two floor cushions and Ryan's favourite fleece blanket.
- **A little hideaway:** Tanmay draped a bedsheet over two chairs to make a small tent. Children love feeling tucked in.
- **A few books:** Just three or four, not the whole shelf. Too many choices at once can be overwhelming.
- **Things to squeeze and watch:** A squishy ball, a soft toy and a glitter calm-down bottle Sophie had made with Tanya the week before.
- **Feelings pictures:** Simple pictures of happy, sad, angry and tired faces, so Ryan could point to how he felt even if he did not have the words.
- **Something for the ears:** A pair of kids' headphones for when the noise got too loud.
- **Some instructions:** Right outside the calm down corner was a simple sign of rules - ask for help from an adult, only 1 person at a time and one tool at a time.

That was it. Simple, quiet and cozy.

## Introducing the calm corner before the party

This is the part most parents skip, and it is the most important one. A calm corner only works if children already know it before they need it. You cannot introduce it in the middle of a meltdown.

So a few days before the dinner, Tanya sat with Sophie and Ryan in the new corner. They read a story there together. They shook the glitter bottle and watched it settle. Tanya told them, "When the party gets too loud or you feel too big inside, you can come here. You can come by yourself or ask me or Papa to come with you."

Sophie loved it so much that she started using it to read every afternoon. Ryan mostly liked the tent. Either way, by the day of the dinner, the corner felt familiar and safe.

## Getting the children ready for the guests

Along with the calm corner, Tanya and Tanmay did a few more things to prepare Sophie and Ryan.

**They talked through the evening.** Who was coming, what they would eat, where the children would play and when bedtime would be. No surprises.

**They put away special toys.** Sophie had a new doll she did not want anyone else touching. Instead of fighting over it during the party, Tanya helped her choose a few toys to keep away in the closet and a few to share with the cousins. Sophie felt heard and there were fewer tears later.

**They gave each child a job.** Sophie was the official greeter. She helped take the guests' coats to the bedroom. Ryan's job was to put everyone's shoes neatly on the shoe mat. He took it very seriously!

**They fed the children first.** The adults' dinner was planned for 7:30 pm. Tanya knew that was too late for hungry little tummies, so Sophie and Ryan ate a proper meal at 6:00 pm right before anyone arrived.

## How the calm corner helped on the night

The first hour went beautifully. Then the house got louder. Uncle Ravi's laugh could be heard from every room. The cousins were running in circles. The doorbell kept ringing.

Around 8:30, Ryan began to whine and pull at Tanya's dress. Then he threw a car at his cousin Zara. Tanya did not scold him in front of everyone. She picked him up and said quietly, "I think your body needs a break. Let's go to your tent."

They sat in the calm corner for about ten minutes. Ryan wore the headphones and watched the glitter bottle. Slowly his breathing slowed down. When Tanya showed him the feelings pictures, he pointed at the tired face. Then, all on his own, he got up and went back to play.

Here is what surprised Tanya the most. Later that evening, she found Sophie sitting in the tent with Zara, reading a book. Sophie had told her cousin, "This is where you go when it's too noisy." Zara had been overwhelmed too, and Sophie had shown her where to go.

The calm corner was not just for Ryan. It became a safe space for every child at the party who needed it.

## Ending the evening without a struggle

Tanya and Tanmay had agreed on one rule before the party: the children's bedtime routine would not be heavily impacted just because there were guests.

At 9:00 pm, Tanmay took Ryan to bed while the party carried on. Same bath, same pyjamas, same two stories. Sophie was allowed to stay up a little longer as she was older, and she went to bed by 10:00 pm. Both children woke up happy the next morning, and so did their parents. That is a win in any family!

## Tips for setting up your own calm corner at home

If you are hosting this holiday season, here is what Tanya and Tanmay would tell you:

- Choose a quiet spot away from the kitchen and the main party area.
- Keep it simple - a cushion, a blanket, a few books and one or two calming things.
- Introduce it a few days early, when your child is calm and happy.
- Never use it as a punishment. Children should want to go there.
- Offer to go with your child. Young toddlers often need you there to calm down.
- Let visiting children use it too. They may need it more than your own!

And the best part? The calm corner does not have to disappear after the holidays. Sophie and Ryan's tent stayed in their room for months.
